# Risk Brief: Large Healthcare Data Breaches

## Headline
Hacking and third-party vendors drive the biggest healthcare breach risk. The typical breach is modest, but a few vendor-related incidents affect enormous numbers of people.

## Three key facts
1. **The average is misleading.** The median breach affects 4,036 people, but the mean is 137,328 because a few mega-breaches (Change Healthcare, about 192.7 million) pull it up. The median describes a typical incident better.
2. **Business associates concentrate the risk.** They are about 15% of breaches (1,244 of 8,087) but about 49% of all people affected. Their median breach is 5,890 people vs. 3,915 for healthcare providers.
3. **Hacking dominates.** Hacking/IT incidents are about 61% of breaches (4,898), with a median of 8,132 people. Every other breach type has a median under 2,200.

## What it means
Most breaches are small, but the biggest harm comes from vendors that hold data for many organizations, and from hacking. Massachusetts looks like the nation overall: 245 breaches (8th among states), with a median of 4,049 vs. 4,035 for the rest of the US. Its mix is mostly providers (167 of 245), whose median is lower (2,739).

## Recommendation
A Massachusetts healthcare organization should put its limited security effort where the data shows the most exposure:
- **Vendor oversight first.** Inventory every business associate that handles patient data. Require security standards and prompt breach notification in contracts, and review the vendors that hold the most records.
- **Defend against hacking.** Hacking is both the most common and the largest breach type, so prioritize protections against network and IT intrusions.
- **Plan for the tail, not the average.** Rare, very large incidents drive total harm, so test incident response and notification plans for a large vendor-caused breach.

## Caveats
- The portal lists only breaches affecting 500 or more people, so smaller incidents are missing.
- 2026 is a partial year (data through 2026-10-16), and HHS can amend records after posting.
- Business associates' 49% share is driven partly by Change Healthcare (about 192.7 million). Without it, their share is lower (see the check below).
- `breach_type_primary` keeps only the first listed breach type.
- Groups like Massachusetts health plans (18 breaches) are too small for firm conclusions.
- This data shows association, not cause. It cannot say why breach sizes rose after 2018.
